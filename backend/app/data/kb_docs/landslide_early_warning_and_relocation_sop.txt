NATIONAL DISASTER MANAGEMENT AUTHORITY & GEOLOGICAL SURVEY GUIDELINES
LANDSLIDE EARLY ACTION, HILLSIDE EVACUATION & SAFE CORRIDOR DIRECTIVES
Document Ref: NDMA/GEO/LS-2024/14 | Classification: Official Guidance

1. HILLSIDE RISK ASSESSMENT THRESHOLDS
Habitations located in hilly terrain (slopes exceeding 25 degrees) are subject to sudden debris flow and toe failure under high rainfall.
- Moderate Alert: Cumulative 48-hour rainfall exceeds 90 mm on slopes > 20 degrees.
- Critical Relocation Alert: Rainfall exceeds 130 mm with slope > 30 degrees, or historical slope reactivation indices exceed 70%.
- Habitations in deep valleys with single-road access must be pre-emptively evacuated during daylight hours to prevent night stranding due to road slippage.

2. NIGHTTIME EVACUATION PROHIBITION
In steep mountain sectors (elevation > 800m), night evacuations across unlit serpentine roads carry a 400% higher fatality risk from falling boulders.
- Authorities must order pre-emptive evacuation before 17:00 hours whenever What-If simulations predict rainfall escalation above 20%.

3. SAFE RELOCATION SHELTER CRITERIA IN HILL DISTRICTS
- Relief camps must NOT be sited at the base of slopes steeper than 15 degrees or in historical alluvial fan runout zones.
- Preferred sites include reinforced multi-story concrete educational institutions, district indoor sports arenas, and hilltop masonry community complexes with independent backup generators and water filtration units.

4. LOGISTICS & ROUTE DEPLOYMENT
- 4WD mini-buses and state transport utility vehicles must be prioritized for hill corridors where turning radius is constrained.
- If a main ghat road segment is flagged as Blocked or Impassable, traffic control posts at both ends must immediately divert all convoys to designated Ridge-line routes.
